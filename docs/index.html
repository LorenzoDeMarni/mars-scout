      How it works

      1. Plan from orbit. NASA's 1 m elevation model marks steep ground and drop-offs before anyone sets out.
      2. Send the scout. At each stop it photographs the ground in up to four directions. Here the scout is a replay of Curiosity's real drive.
      3. Check twice. An AI model trained on NASA's AI4Mars labels classifies the terrain. A separate edge check with no AI looks for rock shadows and smooth sand.
      4. Pick a path. Seven candidate paths are scored in each photo. The safest is green. If the two checks disagree, the stop is never marked clear.
      5. Brief the astronaut. Each stop shows the time to walk back, the weather Curiosity measured there, slope and radiation dose.

      Limits

        • The edge check finds rocks by their shadows and smooth ground by its lack of texture. It has not been scored against labels. It can mistake a shadowed step in bedrock for a rock, and it misses rocks that cast no shadow.
        • The model only judges ground within about 30 m. Dimmed areas of each photo are ignored: the distance, the sky, and rover hardware (which the model recognises itself).
        • Pin colour is the view ahead, toward the next stop, or the safest other view where that photo is unusable.

        NASA data

        • Basemap: MRO HiRISE Curiosity roving site mosaic and MRO CTX Gale Crater mosaic, via Mars Trek
        • Photos and positions: Curiosity navigation cameras and waypoints (NASA/JPL-Caltech)
        • Elevation: MSL Gale DEM mosaic, 1 m, from MRO HiRISE stereo (USGS Astrogeology / NASA PDS)
        • Terrain model training data: AI4Mars (NASA JPL)
        • Weather: Curiosity REMS · Radiation: Curiosity RAD

        Before an astronaut walks on Mars, a scout checks the ground.

        Scout Ahead plans a 1 km Marswalk across the boxwork ridges of Mount Sharp. A robot goes first, looks around at every stop and reports what is safe.

        The map adds steep ground and drop-offs from orbit, weather from Curiosity's own station, radiation dose and the time to walk back.

        Replay, not live. The scout replays a real Curiosity drive (2025–2026). Terrain labels are model predictions, and walking times are estimates.
